## Slide 1 — Cover
- **Headline:** One RFQ. Verified IT vendors. Faster procurement.
- **Takeaway:** Vendra is the structured workspace that replaces email/Excel/WhatsApp IT buying.
- **Content:** Product name + tagline, 5 capability chips (Verified vendors · Structured RFQ · Quote comparison · On-record negotiation · Vendor-led network), founder/round placeholders.
- **Layout:** Dark hero, oversized 3-line headline with gradient middle line, ambient glows + dotted grid. "Working name — swap for [Product Name]" tag top-right.
- **Mockup:** None — typographic hero.
- **Speaker notes:** Lead with the one-liner, not the company. We're pitching a network, not RFQ software. Pre-launch; everything swappable; no fake traction.

## Slide 2 — Why Now
- **Headline:** IT got more complex. Buying it didn't get easier.
- **Takeaway:** Complexity is rising while the buying workflow stays manual — the gap is the opportunity.
- **Content:** Three cards — More to buy / More vendors / Same manual flow. Explicit note that no market-size figure is claimed here.
- **Layout:** Headline + lead, 3-up card grid, disclaimer chip.
- **Mockup:** None.
- **Speaker notes:** Tension slide — complexity up-and-right, workflow flat. Cybersecurity/OEM credentials matter more; price discovery + accountability demanded. TAM deliberately deferred.

## Slide 3 — Problem (fragmented flow)
- **Headline:** IT procurement is still fragmented.
- **Takeaway:** One requirement passes through five disconnected tools over days.
- **Content:** Left — vertical "today" flow (Requirement → email/WhatsApp → search → send RFQ → wait → 5 formats → compare by hand → off-record negotiation → PO → track) each tagged with its failure. Right — 7 problem bullets (slow turnaround, fragmented discovery, unverifiable trust/OEM/certs, mixed formats, manual comparison, off-record negotiation, scattered history, vendor lock-in).
- **Layout:** Two-column; left mono "flow" card with red tags, right coral-dot bullet list.
- **Mockup:** Stylized workflow chain (not a screen).
- **Speaker notes:** Walk the flow top-to-bottom. "Finding a vendor is easy; finding the right vendor is not." No system of record — can't prove best price. This is the wedge.

## Slide 4 — Problem (trust · comparison · negotiation)
- **Headline:** Three things break every time.
- **Takeaway:** Even with vendors in hand, trust, comparison and negotiation each fail structurally — and vendors lose too.
- **Content:** Three cards (Trust: is this vendor real? / Comparison: quotes nobody can compare / Negotiation: vanishes into WhatsApp) + supply-side card (vendors chase invisible opportunities, ready-stock demand unfulfilled).
- **Layout:** 3-up cards + full-width coral-bordered supply-side card with a "?" stat.
- **Mockup:** None.
- **Speaker notes:** Reframe from "slow" to three structural failures. Then flip to supply side to set up a two-sided network.

## Slide 5 — Solution (light breather)
- **Headline:** One procurement workspace. Two sides, one record.
- **Takeaway:** Procurement becomes a structured workflow with verified vendors and everything on one record.
- **Content:** Full pipeline strip (Requirement → Select → **RFQ** → Matched vendors → Quotes → **Compare** → Negotiate → Award → PO → Delivery → Performance) + Buyer journey and Vendor journey chip-flows.
- **Layout:** Light slide; horizontal pipe strip (RFQ + Compare highlighted); two journey cards.
- **Mockup:** Journey chip-flows.
- **Speaker notes:** The pipeline is the spine of the whole product; every later screen is one stage. "One record" = nothing re-keyed, everything auditable.

## Slide 6 — Smart RFQ
- **Headline:** Publish once. Reach the right vendors.
- **Takeaway:** A structured requirement auto-matches to verified vendors who actually carry it, with a clock for urgent ready-stock needs.
- **Content:** RFQ #RFQ-2048 — 10× Dell Latitude 5450, 16GB/512GB, 3-yr warranty, Bangalore, Ready stock, live 30-min countdown, 8 matched verified vendors (Dell Titanium/Gold levels + scores).
- **Layout:** Two-column: RFQ card mockup (left) + matched-vendor list (right).
- **Mockup:** Browser-chrome RFQ screen with **live countdown timer (JS)**, ready-stock tag, matched vendor cards showing partner level + score; note "invite who you know + discover who you don't."
- **Speaker notes:** Structured, not an email paragraph. Matching on capability + verified partner level. Countdown = ready-stock speed. Discovery breaks vendor lock-in and gives new vendors their first at-bat.

## Slide 7 — Quote Comparison
- **Headline:** Quotes nobody wanted to compare — compared for you.
- **Takeaway:** All quotes normalized side-by-side; trade-offs surfaced; cheapest is never blindly "best."
- **Content:** Comparison table — Vendor A/B/C × Unit price, Stock, Delivery, Warranty, Payment, Vendor score. Recommendation chips: Best overall (C) · Best price (B) · Fastest delivery (C) · Best warranty (C). Cheapest (B) is 10 days out → not best overall.
- **Layout:** Full-width browser-chrome table, winning column tinted, recommendation chip row, honesty caption.
- **Mockup:** Normalized comparison grid with tabular-nums, semantic stock tags, recommendation chips.
- **Speaker notes:** Strongest "aha." Point out best-price ≠ best-overall — intellectual honesty is the trust-builder. Buyer still decides.

## Slide 8 — Negotiation
- **Headline:** Negotiation, on the record.
- **Takeaway:** Counter-offers happen in-platform with tracked revisions and measured savings.
- **Content:** Negotiation ladder (₹58,000 → buyer ₹55,000 → vendor ₹56,500 → buyer ₹55,750 → **accepted ₹56,000**). Negotiated savings ₹20,000 (10 units). Tracked fields (original/final, discount, delivery/warranty/terms, validity/stock). AI-assist card (roadmap): "7.8% above comparable," "better pricing above 25 units," "suggested counter ₹55,500" — **buyer stays in control**.
- **Layout:** Two-column: negotiation timeline mockup (left) + savings stat + tracked KV list + dashed AI-assist card (right).
- **Mockup:** Chat-style buyer/vendor bubble ladder ending in green "Accepted."
- **Speaker notes:** Sharpest differentiator — nobody keeps negotiation structured. AI assists, never auto-negotiates. Savings are labelled illustrative demo numbers.

## Slide 9 — Verified Vendor Intelligence + Trust Score
- **Headline:** Trust you can see — not claims on a PDF.
- **Takeaway:** Profiles separate Verified / Vendor-provided / Pending; the Reliability Score is earned, never bought.
- **Content:** Vendor profile (GST, location, years, OEM levels with ✓/pending/provided states, ISO/awards, capabilities) + **91/100 trust ring**. Three-state legend. Reliability Score factor bars (verified OEM/certs, response rate/time, competitiveness, on-time delivery, fulfilment/cancellations, feedback, disputes). Explicit: paid plan unlocks tools, never a higher score.
- **Layout:** Two-column: profile card with conic-gradient score ring (left) + 3-state legend + factor-bar card (right).
- **Mockup:** Vendor profile with credential chips (three verification states) and a conic-gradient score ring.
- **Speaker notes:** Defensible moat + ethical core. Never imply unverified = verified. Score can't be bought. Verification compounds with every completed order.

## Slide 10 — IT Catalog (light)
- **Headline:** One product identity. Every vendor's live terms.
- **Takeaway:** Vendra owns the master spec; vendors attach commercials — so quotes are comparable by construction.
- **Content:** 6 category cards (Computing / Networking / Storage / Cybersecurity / Software / Services with sub-items). "Vendra maintains" (master identity/spec) vs "Vendors maintain" (price, stock, lead time, warranty, discount, terms; CSV/Excel bulk upload then APIs; AI normalization + duplicate-SKU detection).
- **Layout:** Light slide; 6-up category grid + two responsibility cards.
- **Mockup:** Category taxonomy grid.
- **Speaker notes:** Catalog is why comparison works. We own identity, vendors own commercials. AI normalization collapses naming variants. Unglamorous but foundational.

## Slide 11 — The Network (differentiator)
- **Headline:** Not RFQ software. A procurement network.
- **Takeaway:** Software gets used; a network compounds — every participant makes it more valuable.
- **Content:** Node diagram (Vendra core ↔ Businesses, Verified vendors, OEM/distributor ecosystem) + flywheel (more vendors → coverage → buyers → RFQs → vendors). Vision: Businesses ↔ Verified vendors ↔ OEM/distributor ecosystem.
- **Layout:** Two-column: SVG-linked node diagram (left) + flywheel chip-chain card (right).
- **Mockup:** Network graph with gradient core node.
- **Speaker notes:** The investment thesis. "We're building the liquidity layer for B2B IT procurement." Walk the flywheel once. Next slide is the cheap-growth unlock.

## Slide 12 — Vendors Become Our Distribution Channel
- **Headline:** Vendors become our distribution channel.
- **Takeaway:** Vendors bring their own customers, so we don't cold-start demand and CAC collapses.
- **Content:** Branded vendor customer portal (ABC Technologies · 50 corporate customers): invite customers → customers create RFQs → vendor sees them first → other verified vendors compete → network grows. Customers keep control (can invite/discover other vendors). Why a vendor opts in (free portal, first look at RFQs, management tools). "The math."
- **Layout:** Two-column: branded portal mockup (left) + ↓CAC stat + "why a vendor does this" + "the math" card (right).
- **Mockup:** `abc-technologies.vendra.app` branded workspace, "Powered by Vendra."
- **Speaker notes:** The slide investors remember. Solves cold-start demand. Handle the capture objection head-on — buyer always retains control, which keeps RFQs competitive. Vendor trades exclusivity for a free portal + first look.

## Slide 13 — Competitive Landscape (light)
- **Headline:** There's plenty of software. None of it is this.
- **Takeaway:** ITSM manages internal tickets; marketplaces are generic; we're the IT-specialized verified procurement network in between.
- **Content:** 2×2 positioning matrix (Internal ITSM ↔ External procurement · Generic ↔ IT-specialized+verified). Plots ServiceNow/ManageEngine, IndiaMART/GeM, vendor directories, email/Excel/WhatsApp, and **Vendra** (top-right, empty quadrant). Honest side cards. Real incumbent = email+Excel+WhatsApp.
- **Layout:** Light slide; matrix (left) + three positioning cards (right).
- **Mockup:** 2×2 positioning matrix with highlighted "us" marker.
- **Speaker notes:** Never claim no competition. Top-right quadrant is empty = us. Defensibility is the combination (verification + network), not any single feature.

## Slide 14 — Business Model
- **Headline:** Subscriptions now. A network to monetize later.
- **Takeaway:** Two-sided SaaS, free entry both sides; transaction + ecosystem revenue layered on later, never the sole engine.
- **Content:** Buyers — Free / ₹2,999 / ₹7,999 / ₹20,000+ per month. Vendors — Free / Vendor Pro ₹1,999 / Vendor Business ₹4,999+ (multi-user, analytics, customer mgmt, catalog tools, performance analytics). Future revenue chips: transaction fee, verification, enterprise, API, procurement intelligence, OEM/distributor partnerships.
- **Layout:** Two-column pricing grids + dashed "future revenue" card.
- **Mockup:** Pricing cards with mono pricing.
- **Speaker notes:** Free both sides feeds the flywheel; paid tiers monetize depth. Don't lead with take-rate — fragile alone. Prices are a hypothesis to validate with design partners.

## Slide 15 — Go-to-Market
- **Headline:** Land vendors. Let them bring the buyers.
- **Takeaway:** Supply-first, because vendors bring their own demand — no cold-start.
- **Content:** Phase 1 Vendor acquisition (free onboarding: resellers, SIs, OEM partners, distributors, cyber/cloud partners, AMC). Phase 2 Vendor-led buyer acquisition (referral ladder: 5→3mo, 10→6mo, 25→12mo Pro; recurring commissions later). Phase 3 Buyer expansion (procurement/IT/finance, SME, mid-market, multi-location).
- **Layout:** 3 phase columns + green "why supply-first works" banner.
- **Mockup:** Phase timeline with referral-ladder mini-table.
- **Speaker notes:** Operational proof the network isn't hand-wavy. Phase 2 is the crux; ladder is illustrative/tunable. Kills the cold-start-demand objection.

## Slide 16 — Target Market (light)
- **Headline:** Start in India. SMEs and mid-market first.
- **Takeaway:** Companies that buy IT continuously but lack a heavyweight procurement suite; multi-location feels it most.
- **Content:** Sector chips (manufacturing, healthcare, education, BPO/KPO, construction, logistics, retail, professional services, real estate, technology). SME / Mid-market / Multi-location cards. **TAM/SAM/SOM shown as placeholders** — bottom-up SOM to be built, not fabricated.
- **Layout:** Light slide; sectors + 3 segment cards (left) + honest sizing card (right).
- **Mockup:** None (sizing KV list with placeholders).
- **Speaker notes:** India-first, SME+mid-market, multi-location as sharpest wedge. Bottom-up SOM > invented top-down TAM. Any later figure sourced or labelled illustrative.

## Slide 17 — Demo Story + Roadmap/Vision
- **Headline:** 50 laptops in Bangalore. Days → minutes.
- **Takeaway:** The same requirement takes 2–5 days today vs ~30 minutes on Vendra — with savings and an audit trail.
- **Content:** 250-employee Bangalore company needs 50 laptops + networking. Before (2–5 days, no record) vs After (~30 min, savings + audit trail) — **labelled illustrative target, not measured**. Roadmap: MVP (registration, verified profiles, catalog, RFQ, matching, comparison, basic negotiation, dashboards) / V2 (customer portal, bulk upload, AI normalization, advanced scoring, POs, notifications, analytics) / V3 (AI negotiation assistant, price/availability intelligence, ERP, API, mobile, automation). Long-term flow: Requirement → Discovery → RFQ → Quote → Negotiation → Purchase → Delivery → Warranty → Renewal.
- **Layout:** Before/after split + 3-column roadmap.
- **Mockup:** Before/after stat cards.
- **Speaker notes:** Anchor to one memorable story. Clearly illustrative. Roadmap shows sequencing: MVP proves loop, V2 lights the network, V3 is intelligence/integration.
