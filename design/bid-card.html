Issued to the holder. Not publicly resolvable. Shows credentials and validity only — never background-check results. Verification is by scan-and-authorize, in session.

Deliberately constrained. There is no “BGV cleared” badge and there never will be — a portable clearance marker attached to a person is a discriminatory artifact. The single legitimate launch use case is site access: a technician proving current safety certification at a gate. Build for that and nothing more.

The most important rule in the product. Company-provided and BID-verified information are separated visually and structurally, always — distinct panels, distinct backgrounds, explicit headers. A visitor must never be able to mistake a self-written description for a verified fact. If this line is blurred even once, the entire trust proposition is compromised.
No adverse risk findings appear on a public page. A lapsed profile reads “Verification expired — last verified 15 Aug 2026”, neutrally. An unverified entity reads “Not yet verified”. Risk findings go only to authorized requesters under consent — a public “HIGH RISK” badge would be a defamation exposure and a weapon BID has no standing to wield.

Green is reserved exclusively for verification state and is never used decoratively — the moment it appears as a brand accent, it stops carrying meaning. Amber marks expiring, pending and under-review states. Red is reserved for expired, exception and blocked, and is never used for “insufficient evidence”, which is a neutral state.
