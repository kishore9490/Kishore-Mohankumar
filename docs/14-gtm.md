# 14 · Customer Acquisition & Go-To-Market

## 14.1 Who actually buys

Verification is bought by a *function*, not by a company. Selling to "an enterprise" fails; selling to a named role with an owned problem works.

| Function | Their problem | What they buy | Budget | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Procurement / Sourcing** | Vendor onboarding takes weeks; no standard | Speed, standardization, throughput | Yes | **1** |
| **Compliance / Risk** | Cannot evidence diligence to auditors | Audit trail, policy enforcement, monitoring | Yes | **1** |
| **Vendor management** | 800 vendors, no idea which are still valid | Coverage, re-verification, drift reporting | Sometimes | 2 |
| **HR / Talent Acquisition** | BGV is slow, opaque, delays joining | Turnaround, candidate experience, visibility | Yes | 2 |
| **Operations / Plant / EHS** | Contract workers on site, unverified | Worker verification, credential expiry, gate control | Yes | 2 |
| **Finance** | Payment fraud, wrong bank accounts | Bank account ownership verification | Yes | 3 |
| **Legal** | Counterparty due diligence | Ownership, litigation, sanctions | Rarely direct | 3 |
| **Security** | Site and system access by third parties | Identity assurance | Sometimes | 3 |
| **IT** | Vendor security posture | Certification verification | Rarely | 4 |

**Company profile to target:**
- 200–5,000 employees, ₹50 Cr–₹2,000 Cr revenue
- 50+ active vendors, or 100+ contract workers, or 100+ hires per year
- A named procurement or compliance function that already exists — BID cannot create the function *and* sell it a tool
- Regulated, export-exposed, or supplying a large customer that imposes diligence obligations downward

**Highest-intent trigger events** — these convert dramatically better than cold outreach and should drive prospecting:
- Failed or painful audit
- A fraud or fake-vendor incident *(the strongest trigger of all)*
- Losing a customer contract over supply-chain diligence requirements
- A new compliance head or CFO
- Export certification or a large customer's vendor mandate
- A safety incident involving a contractor
- Rapid vendor-base growth after fundraising or expansion

## 14.2 The wedge sequence

Sell one wedge. Expand structurally afterwards.

### Wedge 1 — Vendor / supplier onboarding *(primary)*

**Why this is the right wedge:**
- Clear, budgeted, named owner
- Pain is quantifiable in days and rupees
- The buyer already does the work manually, so no category education is needed
- Produces the vendor invitations that seed the network — **no other wedge does this**
- Highest gross margin in the model ([§13](13-unit-economics.md))

**The opening question:**
> *"How many days does it take your team to verify and onboard a new vendor — from first document request to approved in your system?"*

The answer is usually 10–30 days, and the person answering is usually irritated by it. That irritation is the sale.

**The follow-up:**
> *"BID verifies the business against your own policy, through authorized sources, and gives you an auditable verification record. Your team stops chasing documents. Your auditor stops asking questions you can't answer."*

### Wedge 2 — Candidate & employee BGV

Larger, more competitive, more price-driven, thinner margin. Enter it **after** the vendor wedge is landed inside an account, using the platform relationship rather than a price war. The pitch is consolidation and transparency: one platform, one audit trail, one policy engine for both businesses and people.

### Wedge 3 — Contractor & workforce verification

The highest-volume and least-served opportunity ([§03.7](03-verification-catalog.md#37-contractor--third-party-workforce-verification)). Sells to operations and EHS — a different budget from procurement, which makes it *additive* rather than competitive within the account. Frequently the fastest path to expanding an existing customer.

### Wedge 4 — Continuous third-party risk monitoring

The retention and expansion engine. Rarely the entry point, because nobody has "monitoring" in their budget — but once BID holds the verified vendor base, monitoring is the natural, high-margin, near-automatic upsell. Give it away in the pilot; charge for it at renewal.

## 14.3 The pilot ladder

Land small. Prove. Expand. Never open with an enterprise-wide proposal — it triggers procurement, security review, legal review and a six-month cycle for a company that has never used the product.

```
  10-VENDOR PILOT                 50-VENDOR PILOT              100+ / ANNUAL
  ─────────────────               ───────────────              ────────────
  Paid, 4 weeks                   Paid, 6-8 weeks              Annual contract
  ₹50k–₹1L                        ₹2L–₹4L                      ₹6L–₹25L+
  1 policy, 1 category            2-3 policies                 All categories
  1 champion                      Function-wide                Multi-function
  Success = time saved            Success = adoption           Success = standard
                                    + completion rate            + monitoring live
```

**Charge for the pilot.** A free pilot has no internal owner, no urgency and no budget defence. A ₹50,000 pilot has a person whose credibility depends on it succeeding — and that person becomes the champion. Free pilots are the most common way a promising enterprise deal dies quietly.

**Pilot success criteria, agreed in writing before it starts:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Vendor completion rate | > 80% within 7 days |
| Time from invitation to verified | < 3 days median |
| Reduction vs their current process | > 60% |
| Exceptions correctly surfaced | 100% |
| Champion's stated willingness to expand | Yes/No |

That last row is the only one that actually predicts conversion. Ask it explicitly at the pilot review, and ask what would have to be true if the answer is no.

## 14.4 Phase 1 — Bengaluru and Karnataka

**Why start here:**
- Dense mid-market manufacturing and industrial base, plus an IT/BPO concentration
- Founder-led sales requires physical proximity — enterprise verification is sold in meeting rooms, not over email
- Manageable geography for field verification operations
- Industry associations and CA/CS networks are accessible
- **Regional density drives reuse** ([§10.5](10-reusable-profile.md#105-the-cold-start-problem)) — the same suppliers serve multiple local buyers, which is exactly the condition the network effect needs

**Target sectors for the first 20 customers** — chosen for density and pain, *while the platform stays strictly industry-agnostic*:

| Sector | Why | Primary wedge |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing (auto components, engineering, electronics) | Deep supplier tiers, customer-imposed diligence, contract labour | Vendor + workforce |
| IT / BPO / KPO | High hiring volume, client security mandates | BGV + vendor |
| Healthcare (hospitals, diagnostics) | Regulated, credential-heavy, vendor + staff verification | Vendor + BGV |
| Construction / infrastructure | Heavy contract labour, safety obligations | Workforce |
| Logistics / warehousing | Large third-party workforce, driver verification | Workforce + vendor |
| Facility management | Sells verified workforce as a differentiator to *their* clients | Workforce |
| Pharma | Regulated supply chain, audit-heavy | Vendor (critical) |

**Sequencing rationale:** manufacturing first — it exercises all three wedges in one account, has the densest supplier overlap, and produces the best reference stories.

## 14.5 Channels

### Direct — founder-led, the only channel that matters for the first 20 customers

Nobody else can sell an undefined category. The founder must personally close the first 20 accounts, because every one of those conversations is product research disguised as a sales call. Do not hire a sales team before the pitch, the ICP and the objection set are proven — an AE hired into an unproven motion will fail and will burn a year.

**Motion:** targeted list of 200 companies → warm intro or direct outreach to a named procurement/compliance head → problem-discovery call, not a demo → tailored demo using *their* vendor names → 10-vendor paid pilot → expansion.

### Partner channels — build from month 6, meaningful from month 12

| Channel | Why they help | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **Procurement consultants** | Already inside procurement transformations | Referral fee, a tool that makes their advice actionable |
| **CA / CS firms** | Trusted by exactly the mid-market target; already do compliance work | Referral revenue, a client-retention tool |
| **HR consultants & staffing firms** | Own the BGV conversation | Margin on BGV, faster placements |
| **BGV agencies** | Potential partners *and* competitors — use BID as their orchestration layer | White-label capability, better margins |
| **ERP / SAP / Oracle implementation partners** | Sit inside vendor master-data projects | Integration services revenue |
| **HRMS partners** | Own the hiring workflow | Embedded verification, revenue share |
| **Compliance consultants** | Called in exactly when a diligence failure happens | A remediation tool to recommend |
| **Industry associations** | Credibility and access at scale | Member benefit, sponsored programmes |
| **MSPs / facility management firms** | Verified workforce is their sales differentiator | Bulk verification, white-label |

**Priority order:** CA/CS networks and industry associations first (cheapest access to the mid-market), then procurement and HR consultants, then ERP/HRMS partners (highest value, longest to activate).

**Partner discipline:** a partner programme with no enablement, no deal registration and no first reference win is a distraction. Do not launch one until three partners have each closed a deal with founder support.

### Product-led — the supply-side loop

Every invited vendor is a product-led acquisition surface ([§10](10-reusable-profile.md)). A verified vendor that shares its BID ID with other buyers generates inbound. This is slow to start and compounds — instrument it from day one so its contribution is visible, because it will be invisible in the first year and material in the third.

## 14.6 Marketing

Given a technical, credibility-driven category, marketing should build **authority**, not awareness.

**Works:**
- Sector-specific content: *"What a critical supplier verification policy should contain for auto component manufacturers"*
- Benchmark research: *"How long does vendor onboarding actually take in Indian mid-market manufacturing?"* — original data BID can generate from its own platform, in aggregate and anonymized, and nobody else has it
- Templates and tools: free policy templates, a vendor onboarding time calculator
- Fraud and incident case studies (anonymized, with permission)
- Regulatory explainers on DPDP, contract labour obligations, supply-chain diligence
- Industry association webinars and speaking slots

**Does not work:** generic SaaS content, broad brand advertising, feature-led messaging, anything implying government affiliation.

**Constraint:** every piece of marketing must respect [§07.4](07-bid-card-and-profile.md)'s verified/unverified distinction and [§09.6](09-risk-assessment.md#96-language-discipline)'s language rules. Marketing overreach is a direct threat to the trust proposition — a single ad implying BID is government-backed would be worse than a quarter of missed pipeline.

## 14.7 The first 90 days

| Weeks | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Build a 200-company target list; map named owners | Qualified list |
| 1–4 | 30 problem-discovery conversations — **no pitching**, only listening | Validated pain, real onboarding timelines, price sensitivity |
| 3–6 | Secure provider quotations in parallel | Real costs into the model ([§13](13-unit-economics.md)) |
| 4–8 | Close 3 design-partner pilots at pilot pricing | 3 paying pilots |
| 6–12 | Run pilots; instrument everything, especially vendor completion | Cost per verification, completion rate, time saved |
| 10–12 | Convert 2 of 3 to annual contracts; capture reference stories | 2 references, validated pricing |

**The three numbers that decide whether to continue as planned:** vendor completion rate, actual cost per verification, and whether a pilot customer will pay an annual fee without a discount. Everything else is secondary.

## 14.8 Metrics

| Stage | Metric | Early target |
|---|---|---|
| Pipeline | Discovery calls per week | 10 |
| Pipeline | Discovery → pilot conversion | > 15% |
| Sales | Pilot → annual conversion | > 60% |
| Sales | Sales cycle | < 90 days mid-market |
| **Product** | **Vendor completion rate** | **> 80% in 7 days** |
| Product | Median time to verified | < 3 days |
| Product | Verifications per account per month | Growing |
| Economics | Cost per verification | Trending down |
| Economics | Gross margin | > 70% blended |
| Economics | CAC payback | < 12 months |
| Network | Entities in the graph | Compounding |
| Network | Reuse rate | > 30% by year 2 |
| Retention | Net revenue retention | > 120% |

**Vendor completion rate is the leading indicator for everything.** If vendors do not complete, campaigns stall, customers churn, the network never forms and the cost model breaks. It should be on the wall.
